Gallery Wall Ideas: How to Plan and Hang a Poster Wall

Updated 2026-11-14

A good gallery wall looks effortless, but it is almost always planned. Choose one layout, keep one thing consistent (frames, colours or a series), keep the gaps equal, and hang the whole group as if it were one picture. Here is how to do each step.

1. Pick a layout

Four layouts cover nearly every wall:

  • The grid. Four, six or nine prints in the same size, in straight rows. Calm, modern and the easiest to get right — ideal for a series such as botanical plates or travel posters.
  • The row. Two to four prints side by side, aligned along the top or the centre line. Perfect above a sofa, a sideboard or a bed.
  • The anchor. One large print (50×70 or 70×100 cm) with smaller 30×40 cm prints around it. The big piece carries the wall; the small ones add story.
  • The salon wall. Many sizes, filled edge to edge in the style of a Victorian exhibition hall. The most atmospheric layout, and the one that suits steampunk best — but it needs the most planning.

2. Choose what ties it together

A gallery wall falls apart when everything varies at once. Keep at least one of these constant:

  • Frames — all black, all dark wood, or all brass-toned.
  • Palette — warm sepia, brass and teal, for example, repeated in every print.
  • Theme — one story told across several pieces: an airship crew, a clockwork herbarium, a run of engineering drawings.

Series that are drawn to belong together are the shortcut here. The scenes in a set share a palette and a mood, so they sit side by side without any extra work.

3. Spacing and height

Use 5–8 cm between frames on most walls, and keep that gap the same everywhere — equal spacing matters more than equal sizes. Treat the whole group as one picture: its centre should sit about 145–150 cm from the floor, or 20–30 cm above a piece of furniture. The whole group should be roughly two-thirds the width of the furniture below it.

4. Plan it on the floor, then on paper

  1. Lay the framed prints out on the floor in front of the wall and move them until the arrangement feels balanced. Start with the largest piece and build outwards.
  2. Take a photo of the arrangement from above.
  3. Cut a sheet of paper to the size of each frame, mark where the hook will go, and tape the papers to the wall in the same arrangement.
  4. Live with the paper version for a day. Adjust until it looks right from the doorway and from where you sit.
  5. Drive the nails or hooks straight through the marks on the paper, then tear the paper away.

This takes an extra half hour and saves a wall full of misplaced holes.

5. Mix sizes the easy way

If you are mixing sizes, stay inside the standard poster formats — 30×40, 50×70 and 70×100 cm — so frames are easy to buy and the proportions relate to each other. A dependable starter set is one 50×70 cm print flanked by two pairs of 30×40 cm prints. Our poster size guide explains which size works where.

Gallery wall ideas by room

  • Living room: an anchor layout above the sofa, with one statement print and four smaller pieces.
  • Study or workshop: a grid of engineering drawings, blueprints or mechanical studies — order and precision suit a room for thinking.
  • Hallway: a single row at eye height, read as you walk past, like frames in a corridor of an old museum.
  • Bedroom: a calm row of two or three botanical prints above the bed.
  • Stairs: follow the slope, keeping each frame’s centre at the same height above its step.

Steampunk gallery walls

Steampunk is made for the salon layout: dark walls, warm frames and a mix of scenes, specimens and diagrams, as if the wall belonged to an inventor’s study. Add a real object or two — a pocket watch on a hook, a pair of goggles, a brass compass — and the wall stops being decoration and starts telling a story.
