The genre, in three reads

Steampunk is Victorian engineering that kept going: brass, steam, goggles, airships. It is a design language more than a rulebook, which is why the same vocabulary stretches from a formal tailcoat to a t-shirt print.

  • Steampunk vs. dieselpunk — how to tell the two apart and pick one.
  • What is dieselpunk? — the 1930s and 1940s branch: chrome, Bakelite, Art Deco.
  • What is gaslamp fantasy? — the softer, more magical Victorian cousin.

If you want something to read rather than wear, the steampunk reading guide covers a book for every kind of reader, and our own short stories are free: The Clockwork Herbarium, The Clockwork Seer and The Last Flight of the Meridian.

The five-piece rule

Almost every look on this site can be rebuilt from five pieces. Own these and you can improvise:

  1. A corset or corset belt — the anchor.
  2. One styled layer — tee, hoodie or tailcoat.
  3. Goggles — on your hat, around your neck, or pushed up on your head.
  4. One brass accessory — pocket watch, ring or coat buttons.
  5. Shoes you can actually walk in — boots beat heels at cons.
