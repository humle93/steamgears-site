For the naturalist: the Clockwork Herbarium

Some steampunk fans are less interested in airships than in what a brass world would do to nature. The Clockwork Herbarium prints are drawn like plates from an old natural history cabinet: a rose with a clockwork heart, a luna moth with hammered brass wings and a raven built around a chronometer. They are calm enough for a living room and strange enough to start a conversation, and two or three side by side make a small gallery wall. The story behind the series is told in The Clockwork Herbarium.

Gift it: one print for a desk or bedroom, all three for someone who is redecorating. See the whole Mechanical Nature series.

For the airship dreamer: the Raven Chronometer

Every steampunk group has one person who talks about airships the way other people talk about football. The Raven Chronometer is the print for them: a raven perched on a pocket chronometer and a stack of old books, engraved in the same brass-and-teal palette as the rest of the series. It reads as navigation and machinery without a single airship in the frame — which is why it also works in a hallway or an office.

Gift it: pair it with the Clockwork Rose for a two-print wall, or read the poster size guide before choosing a frame.

For the everyday steampunk: the Brass Luna Moth

Not everyone wants a full costume on the wall either. The Brass Luna Moth is the quiet one: a single moth with brass wings and dried thistles, warm enough for a bedroom and formal enough for a study. It is the print that fits a room that is not otherwise steampunk — one brass detail, no costume.

Gift it: it is the safest of the three if you do not know the recipient’s taste in machinery; the moth reads as natural history first and steampunk second.

For the one who has everything: all three

The Clockwork Herbarium was drawn as a set, and the three prints share a palette: tarnished gold, muted red and deep teal. Hung together they read as one cabinet of curiosities rather than three separate pictures. Three prints come to 1 185 kr., and a row of three same-size frames is the cheapest way to make a wall look deliberate.
