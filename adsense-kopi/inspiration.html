Start with a character, not a costume

A costume is a list of items. A character is a decision about who you are for the evening, and once that is made every later choice gets easier — the coat or the waistcoat, goggles or a monocle, whether your hands are empty or holding a tool.

We keep five character lines in the shop, each assembled from pieces that already work together: the airship captain, the Victorian inventor, the dark clockwork figure, the mechanical explorer, and the beginner who wants three items and no sewing.

  • Airship captain — leather, brass and a coat that moves
  • Victorian inventor — waistcoat, watch chain, ink
  • Dark clockwork — black, brass and almost no colour
  • Mechanical explorer — canvas, dust, field kit
  • Beginner steampunk — three pieces, nothing to sew

Three registers of the same century

Clockwork, diesel and gaslamp are three different answers to the same question about machinery: is it made of parts you can name, is it a machine you can drive, or is it a rule the world obeys whether you like it or not? They come with different palettes and different decades.

The mistake is mixing them without knowing why. Brass beside chrome reads as a muddle; brass beside dark oak reads as a decision. Steampunk and dieselpunk side by side is the page that explains where the line sits, and what is dieselpunk goes deeper into the half of the genre with rivets instead of gears.

  • What is dieselpunk? — the 1930s that never happened

Three recipes to steal

Not full costumes — three ways of thinking about one, each of which changes what you buy next.

  • One brass detail. Everything else stays modern and dark. The single metal object is what people remember, and it is the cheapest way to look deliberate.
  • Work clothes first. Start from what a person in a workshop would actually wear — canvas, wool, a shirt with the sleeves rolled — and add the fantasy last. It reads as a character rather than as a party.
  • Nothing in your hands. A costume without a prop forces the silhouette to do the work. If it still reads as steampunk with empty hands, it will read twice as well with a spanner.
