Steampunk Corset Guide: Underbust vs Overbust, Fit & Styling

Updated 2026-11-15

Few garments define the look quite like a steampunk corset. It sharpens the silhouette, anchors every accessory you add and instantly says “airship captain” rather than “costume shop.” Yet many first-time buyers end up with a corset that pinches, gapes or simply does not suit the outfit they had in mind.

This guide walks you through the whole process: the difference between underbust and overbust styles, how to measure yourself properly, how to lace and break in a new corset, and how to style it into a complete look. Gather a tape measure and let us begin.

Steampunk Corset Basics: Underbust vs Overbust

Every corset falls into one of two main families. Understanding them is the first decision you will make, and it shapes everything that follows.

The underbust corset

An underbust corset starts just below the bust and ends around the hips. It shapes the waist without covering the chest, so you wear it over a blouse, shirt or dress.

  • Best for: layered outfits, adventurer and engineer characters, and anyone new to corsetry.
  • Why people love it: it is versatile, easier to fit, and works with many different tops.
  • Keep in mind: it offers no bust support, so the blouse or bra underneath matters.

The overbust corset

An overbust corset covers the bust as well as the waist, often from just under the armpits down to the hips. It can be worn as a top in its own right or layered over a chemise.

  • Best for: dramatic evening looks, Victorian aristocrat and sky-pirate characters, and outfits where the corset is the star.
  • Why people love it: a bold, statement silhouette with built-in bust shaping.
  • Keep in mind: fit is more demanding, because the bust measurement must be right as well as the waist.

What You Will Need

Before measuring or lacing, gather these items:

  • A soft fabric tape measure
  • A notebook or phone to record measurements
  • A mirror (a full-length one is ideal)
  • A thin, close-fitting layer to wear underneath, such as a cotton chemise, vest or blouse
  • A friend to help with lacing (optional, but very welcome)
  • The size chart supplied with the corset you are considering

Step-by-Step: How to Choose and Fit Your Steampunk Corset

  1. Decide on your look first. Picture the finished outfit. Will the corset sit over a blouse with a jacket on top, or will it be the main event? Layered, practical looks lean towards underbust; dramatic stand-alone looks lean towards overbust.
  2. Measure your natural waist. Find the narrowest part of your torso, usually just above the navel. Stand relaxed, breathe normally and keep the tape snug but not tight. Do not hold your breath or pull in your stomach.
  3. Measure your underbust. Wrap the tape directly beneath the bust, where a bra band would sit. This matters for both styles, since it is where an underbust starts and where an overbust needs to grip.
  4. Measure your bust (overbust only). Measure around the fullest part of the bust while wearing the bra or layer you plan to wear underneath.
  5. Measure your hips and torso length. Measure around the hips where the corset will end, then measure vertically from under the bust to your lap while seated. A corset that is too long will dig in when you sit down.
  6. Compare with the size chart. Every maker sizes differently, so always use the chart for the specific corset rather than your usual dress size. Many corsetieres size by waist measurement, and the corset is designed to close smaller than your natural waist. If you fall between sizes, read the product notes carefully or contact the seller before ordering.
  7. Check the lacing gap. Once the corset arrives, lace it up. A gap of roughly two to three fingers at the back is a common sign of a good fit. A fully closed back on day one often means the corset is too big; a very wide gap suggests it is too small.
  8. Lace it the right way. Loosen the laces completely, fasten the front busk (if it has one) or wrap it around you, then tighten gradually from the top and bottom towards the loops at the waist. Make several gentle passes rather than one hard pull.
  9. Break it in gently. Wear a new corset loosely for short sessions at first, gradually increasing time and snugness. This lets the materials mould to your body and reduces stress on seams and boning.
  10. Listen to your body. A well-fitted corset feels like a firm hug. Sharp pinching, numbness or difficulty breathing mean it is too tight. Loosen it straight away.

A corset should make you stand taller, not hold your breath. Comfort is what lets you wear it through an entire convention day.

How to Style a Steampunk Corset

The corset is the backbone of the outfit, but the layers around it tell the story. Here are proven combinations to build from.

The adventurer

Pair an underbust corset with a loose white or cream blouse, rolled sleeves and practical trousers or a split skirt. Add a utility belt, sturdy boots and goggles pushed up on your forehead. Earthy browns and brass tones keep it grounded.

The Victorian aristocrat

An overbust corset with a full bustle or layered skirt creates a formal, evening-ready silhouette. Finish with lace gloves, a small top hat and a pocket watch pendant. Deep reds, blacks and golds feel suitably opulent.

The sky pirate

Combine a leather-look corset with a long coat or tailcoat, a sash and tall boots. Throw in a few mismatched brass accessories for a well-travelled, slightly roguish air.

Everyday steampunk

An underbust worn over a simple fitted shirt and a dark skirt or jeans gives a subtle nod to the aesthetic that works outside conventions. Keep accessories minimal: one statement necklace or a pair of fingerless gloves is enough.

Common Fitting Mistakes to Avoid

  • Buying by dress size. Always measure and use the specific size chart.
  • Lacing too tight too soon. Patience during break-in gives a better fit and a longer-lasting corset.
  • Ignoring torso length. A corset that is too long will push into your hips or ribs when you sit.
  • Skipping the under-layer. A thin layer underneath protects your skin and the corset lining from sweat and friction.
  • Storing it folded. Hang it or lay it flat to protect the boning and shape.

Caring for Your Corset

Air your corset after each wear by hanging it somewhere dry and ventilated. Spot clean rather than machine wash unless the maker states otherwise, and follow any material-specific care instructions, since leather, faux leather and fabric corsets all need different treatment. FAQ

Is an underbust or overbust corset better for beginners?

Underbust corsets are usually easier for beginners because they only need to fit the waist and hips, and they work with many different tops. Overbust corsets also require a correct bust fit.

How tight should a steampunk corset be?

It should feel like a firm hug. You should be able to breathe, sit and move comfortably. Pinching, numbness or shortness of breath mean it is too tight and should be loosened.

What should I wear under a corset?

A thin, close-fitting layer such as a cotton chemise, vest or blouse protects your skin and the corset lining. With an underbust, the blouse or bra underneath also provides bust support.

How do I know which corset size to order?

Measure your natural waist, underbust, bust (for overbust styles), hips and torso length, then compare them to the size chart for that specific corset. Do not rely on your usual dress size.

Do I need to break in a new corset?

Yes. Wear it loosely for short periods at first and gradually increase wearing time and snugness. This helps it shape to your body and protects seams and boning.
