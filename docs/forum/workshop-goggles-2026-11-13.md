## Brødtekst (HTML)

```html
<p>Almost every cheap pair of steampunk goggles gives itself away in the same three places, and none of them
is the colour. The colour is easy. It is the surface, the lens and the strap.</p>

<h2>The three tells</h2>
<p><strong>1. A surface with no history.</strong> Real brass is never one even tone. It is brighter where a
thumb has worn it and darker in the seam where nobody touches it. A moulded frame has the same finish
everywhere, including the inside of the eye cup, which is the part you never see and the part a manufacturer
never bothers to weather.</p>
<p><strong>2. A lens with no thickness.</strong> Look at the edge of the lens where it meets the rim. Glass and
cast acrylic show a visible band and a slight change in colour at the edge. A thin, perfectly flat disc with a
mirror coating reflects the room in one hard sheet, and that reflection is what reads as toy.</p>
<p><strong>3. A strap that was chosen for cost.</strong> Thin elastic webbing with a plastic slide buckle is the
single fastest way to make an otherwise good pair look like it came from a party bag. Leather, or a cotton
webbing wide enough to stay flat, changes the whole object.</p>

<h2>What the four parts are actually made of</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Frame:</strong> brass-plated steel, painted ABS, or EVA foam sealed and painted. Plated steel ages
well and can be re-polished; ABS is lighter and easier to reshape with heat; foam is the cheapest route to a
custom shape and the least durable at the hinge.</li>
<li><strong>Lens:</strong> polycarbonate or cast acrylic, 2–3 mm. Smoke, amber and pale green all read as
period. Mirror coatings do not: they belong to sunglasses, not to an airship.</li>
<li><strong>Strap:</strong> 2 mm veg-tanned leather with a brass roller buckle, or 25 mm cotton webbing. Elastic
only belongs on a pair you wear for ten minutes in a photograph.</li>
<li><strong>Hardware:</strong> solid brass or nickel-plated eyelets and rivets. "Brass effect" is paint over
steel, and it chips exactly where the strap pulls.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Four things to check before you pay</h2>
<ol>
<li>Open and close the hinge ten times. If it loosens in your hand, it will loosen on a costume.</li>
<li>Press the lens from the inside. If it flexes, it will pop out in a crowd.</li>
<li>Look for a parting line on the frame. A visible seam down the middle of the eye cup cannot be hidden, only
painted over, and paint over a seam looks like paint over a seam.</li>
<li>Check whether the lenses are replaceable. A pair with a threaded retaining ring is worth twice a glued
pair, because you can change the tint without changing the goggles.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Three fixes that take one evening</h2>
<p><strong>Dry-brush the edges.</strong> A barely-loaded brush in dark bronze along the seams, the inside of the
eye cup and the hinge, then a second pass in brass on the high points. Two thin passes, not one thick one.</p>
<p><strong>Kill the shine.</strong> Matte varnish over a glossy frame. New plastic reflects evenly and evenly is
the tell; matte scatters light and reads as cast metal.</p>
<p><strong>Change the strap.</strong> The cheapest improvement per minute in this whole hobby. Cut a leather
strip, punch two holes, fit a brass buckle, and the same pair of goggles moves up a category.</p>

<h2>So: build or buy?</h2>
<p>Build if you enjoy the evening and want a shape nobody else has. Buy if the pair will be worn more than a
dozen times: the hinge and the lens retention are genuinely hard to make well at a kitchen table. The full
comparison is in our <a href="https://steamgears.eu/diy-vs-buy-steampunk-goggles/">DIY vs. buy breakdown</a>.</p>
<p>If you are weathering a pair this week, the five techniques that survive outdoor photography are in
<a href="https://steamgears.eu/build/weathering/">weathering and ageing</a>, and the short list of tools you
actually need is in <a href="https://steamgears.eu/build/tools-and-materials/">tools and materials</a>.</p>
<p>What is the pair you kept after trying four? I am still looking for a hinge that survives a full convention
weekend.</p>
```
